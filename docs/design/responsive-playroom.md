# Board-first responsive playroom
The board stays in the original six-unit coordinate system with a thinner 6.3-unit frame. Camera framing prioritizes board size and permits overlap. All pieces are present without pagination. Landscape uses side trays and portrait uses a bottom scatter area. Seeded jittered grids maximize minimum spacing, with deterministic vertical stacking and nearest-hit raycasting. Removing an upper piece reveals pieces beneath it. A picked piece rises above the maximum stack; failed placements return home. Successful placements preserve other resting positions. Resize reflows only resting pieces when idle.
The active play screen has one compact header with back, title, optional hint and sound, followed by a thin nonnumeric progress line. No reset, shuffle, remaining count, bottom toolbar or footer. Replay and retry remain available only on completion and failure screens. The picture guide is a dismissible overlay. No new dependencies. QA: 4/9/25 pieces, 320x568 and 390x844, landscape, overlapping hit targets, orientation changes, hint, cancellation and retry. Browser measurements pending.

## Gameplay feel
Snap uses a square capture zone extending 80% of a cell in each axis (previously 48%); correct target centers stay unchanged. Capture zones intentionally overlap, but only the selected piece's own target is tested, and the center of an adjacent cell remains outside the tolerance. Resting pieces use shared tray/geometry heights, a thin .08 extrusion and .012 bevel, and conservative overlap-based stacking rather than global elevation by piece index. Disjoint pieces sit on the tray, and remaining stacks settle vertically after a placement without moving their XZ homes. Pickup height is at least 1.8 world units with .65 clearance above the highest other piece (including thickness), creating a more visible detached shadow while selected without changing resting shadows. Light is closer to vertical with lower normal bias to shorten contact shadows. Visual tuning requires browser QA.
